3D Protostellar Evolution in First Star Formation

Understanding how protostars evolve under gas accretion is essential for determining the final properties of the first stars. However, in most previous simulations of first star formation, protostars were treated as sink particles or subgrid models, and their internal evolution was not explicitly resolved. To overcome this limitation, we develop a new radiation hydrodynamics scheme and perform large-scale simulations on supercomputers to directly follow the 3D evolution of accreting protostars.
Papers: Kimura, K., Hosokawa, T. Sugimura, K. & Fukushima, H., 2023, The Astrophysical Journal, 950, 184
     Kimura, K., Sugimura, K. Hosokawa, T., Fukushima H., & Omukai K., 2025, arXiv:2510.13949

Ly α Feedback on Accretion

Lyα photons have an extremely large scattering cross section with neutral hydrogen, causing them to undergo numerous resonant scatterings in the primordial gas. As a result, Lyα photons escaping from ionized regions around black holes or stars can exert strong radiation pressure on the ambient medium, potentially suppressing gas accretion and limiting the growth of these objects. To investigate this effect quantitatively, I am developing a numerical code in collaboration with Aaron Smith, based on the framework of Smith et al. (2018).

Massive Black Hole Formation in Stroing X-ray Environments

Massive black holes formed in the early Universe are considered plausible progenitors of supermassive black holes residing in galactic centers and those observed at high redshifts by JWST. However, recent 21-cm observations by HERA suggest that X-ray backgrounds are stronger than expected in the early Universe, which can suppress the formation of massive black holes. Motivated by this, I have investigated how much massive black holes can form under strong X-ray irradiation using semi-analytic models.
Papers : Kimura, K., Inayoshi, K. & Omukai, K., 2025, The Astrophysical Journal, 990, 228
